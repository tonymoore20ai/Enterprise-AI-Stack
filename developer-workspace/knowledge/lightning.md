# Lightning and thunderstorm safety

Source: https://www.weather.gov/safety/lightning-tips
Source: https://www.weather.gov/media/twc/LightningSafety-OnePager-11-29-18.pdf

## The basic rule
No place outdoors is safe when a thunderstorm is nearby. If you can hear thunder, you are close enough to be struck by lightning. Go to a safe shelter as soon as you hear thunder.

## What counts as safe shelter
- A substantial building with plumbing or electrical wiring (not a picnic shelter, tent, shed or dugout).
- A fully enclosed vehicle with a hard metal roof and the windows closed.

## When it is safe to go back outside: the 30-minute rule
Stay inside until 30 minutes have passed since the last thunder you heard. Every new rumble restarts the 30-minute count.

## Indoors during a storm
- Avoid corded phones, computers and anything else plugged into the mains.
- Avoid plumbing: do not shower, bathe or wash dishes.
- Stay away from windows, doors and porches.
- Do not lie on concrete floors or lean against concrete walls.

## If you are caught outside with no shelter
These steps only lower the risk a little; getting to shelter is what keeps you safe.
- Get off hills, ridges and peaks right away.
- Never shelter under a lone tree or a rock overhang.
- Do not lie flat on the ground.
- Get out of and away from ponds, lakes and other water immediately.
- Keep away from things that conduct electricity, such as wire fences, power lines and windmills.
