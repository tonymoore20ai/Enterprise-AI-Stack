# Heat safety

Source: https://www.weather.gov/media/twc/HeatSafety-OnePager.pdf
Source: https://www.weather.gov/safety/heat-illness

## Heat index and heat alerts
The heat index combines air temperature and humidity into how hot it feels. For example, 95°F at 50% relative humidity feels like 105°F. The National Weather Service uses four risk levels for heat illness during long exposure or hard activity: Caution, Extreme Caution, Danger and Extreme Danger.
- Excessive Heat Watch: dangerous heat is possible in the next 2 to 5 days.
- Excessive Heat Warning: extremely dangerous heat is expected within 1 to 3 days.
- Heat Advisory: dangerous heat is expected within 1 to 3 days, at a lower level than a warning.

## Staying cool
- Drink water regularly, even before you feel thirsty. Avoid alcohol and caffeine. People on fluid-restricted diets should ask a doctor first. Do not take salt tablets unless a doctor recommends them.
- Move strenuous activity to the coolest part of the day, usually early morning or evening.
- Wear lightweight, loose, light-coloured clothing and limit direct sun; sunburn makes it harder for the body to cool itself.
- Use air conditioning, or spend the hottest hours in cooled public places such as libraries or malls.
- A fan alone does not cool a room. Above about 90°F, do not point a fan straight at yourself: the dry airflow dehydrates you faster.
- Eat light meals and keep meat and dairy in a cooler, as they spoil quickly in heat.

## Children, older adults, pets and cars
- Never leave a child, older adult, disabled person or pet in a parked car, even for a few minutes. Dozens of children and many pets die in hot vehicles every year.
- Check often on infants, older adults, people who are ill and pets, and keep them in the coolest place available.

## Heat cramps
Painful muscle spasms, often in the legs or stomach, with heavy sweating. Press on or gently massage the muscle and give small sips of water unless the person feels sick. Get medical help if cramps last more than an hour.

## Heat exhaustion
Signs: heavy sweating, weakness, cool pale clammy skin, fast weak pulse, dizziness, nausea or vomiting, headache, fainting.
What to do: move the person somewhere cooler, ideally air-conditioned; loosen clothing; apply cool wet cloths or a cool bath; give sips of water. Get medical help right away if they vomit, symptoms get worse, or symptoms last more than an hour.

## Heat stroke (emergency)
Signs: body temperature above 103°F, hot red skin that may be dry or damp, fast strong pulse, throbbing headache, confusion, slurred speech, dizziness, fainting or loss of consciousness.
What to do: call 911 immediately; delay can be fatal. Move the person somewhere cooler and cool them with wet cloths or a bath. Do not give them anything to drink. Use a fan only if the heat index is below the high 90s °F.
