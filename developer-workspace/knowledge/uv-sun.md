# UV index and sun safety

Source: https://www.epa.gov/node/3627
Source: https://19january2017snapshot.epa.gov/sunsafety/uv-index-scale-1_.html

## The UV index scale
The UV index runs from 0 (low) to 11+ (extreme) and forecasts how strong the sun's ultraviolet radiation will be.
- 0 to 2, Low: minimal danger for the average person; wear sunglasses on bright days.
- 3 to 5, Moderate: take precautions and seek shade around midday.
- 6 to 7, High: protection is needed; reduce time in the sun between 10 am and 4 pm.
- 8 to 10, Very High: extra protection; unprotected skin can burn quickly.
- 11 and above, Extreme: take all precautions; unprotected skin can burn in minutes.
EPA issues a UV Alert when the UV index is forecast to be 6 or higher and unusually high for the time of year.

## Protecting yourself
- Limit time in the sun between 10 am and 4 pm.
- Seek shade, and wear protective clothing, a wide-brimmed hat and UV-blocking sunglasses.
- Use broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 15 or higher, applied generously; reapply every 2 hours and after swimming or sweating, even on cloudy days.
- Take extra care near water, sand and snow, which reflect UV and increase exposure.
