# Air quality and smoke

Source: https://document.airnow.gov/air-quality-index-aqi-basics.pdf
Source: https://www.epa.gov/node/232073

## The Air Quality Index (AQI)
The AQI runs from 0 to 500; higher numbers mean more pollution and greater health concern. An AQI of 100 matches the national air quality standard for that pollutant.
- 0 to 50, Good (green): air quality is satisfactory, with little or no risk.
- 51 to 100, Moderate (yellow): acceptable, but people who are unusually sensitive to air pollution may be affected.
- 101 to 150, Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups (orange): sensitive groups may feel health effects; the general public is less likely to.
- 151 to 200, Unhealthy (red): anyone may feel health effects, and sensitive groups more seriously.
- 201 to 300, Very Unhealthy (purple): health alert, everyone's risk is higher.
- 301 and above, Hazardous (maroon): emergency conditions; everyone is more likely to be affected.

## On poor air quality or smoky days
- Check the AQI forecast and the current AQI before going out, and plan outdoor time for when the air is cleaner.
- Above 100, reduce strenuous outdoor activity such as running or mowing, starting with sensitive groups.
- In heavy smoke, stay indoors or go to a cleaner-air shelter.
- If you start coughing, wheezing or feel short of breath, cut your exposure right away.
